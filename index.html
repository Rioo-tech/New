Rule to rememberTrap that costs marksKey term

Depreciation

Straight line charges the same amount every year. Written down value charges a percentage of the reducing balance, so the asset never reaches zero. Always read the purchase and sale dates first, because part-year depreciation is where marks quietly leak.

Final Accounts

A trial balance item appears once. An adjustment appears twice, once in the Trading or Profit and Loss Account and once in the Balance Sheet. Miss the second place and the Balance Sheet will not tally.

Rectification of Errors

If the trial balance still tallies, no Suspense Account is needed. Only a one-sided error disturbs it, and that is when Suspense Account becomes the bridge. Before you journalise, write the wrong entry and the correct entry side by side.

Inventory

Value stock at cost or net realisable value, whichever is lower. If stock was counted on a different date, roll it forward or back using the purchases and sales in between, and convert sales to cost first.

Bills of Exchange

Due date is acceptance date plus term plus 3 days of grace. Count it on a calendar, because a wrong due date spoils every entry after it. If the bill is dishonoured, the drawee’s account is debited again.

Bank Reconciliation

Cash Book is your record and Pass Book is the bank’s. Split every item into two heaps: those that need a Cash Book entry and those that are only timing differences. If you start from an overdraft, every sign flips.

Not-for-Profit Organisations

Receipts and Payments is a cash summary. Income and Expenditure is the profit statement, and it leaves out capital items. Subscriptions go on an accrual basis: add what is due, remove what is received in advance.
